A calendar is more than arithmetic. It is a shared way to mark festivals, seasonal change, ceremonies, and the passage from one generation to the next. In community notes, **Vijayaptham** is linked to a count that begins around Chithirai and uses 1312 as a point of reference.

The underlying calculation is simple:

> **2026 − 1312 = 714**

That arithmetic produces 714. Whether a community year labeled 714 corresponds to all of 2026 depends on when its calendar turns over. If the year changes around mid-April, the same count spans parts of two Gregorian years.

## One date, several calendars

The Tamil month of Chithirai begins around April. The exact date varies across years, and calendar conventions matter. A useful record distinguishes a proposed start date, the source that documents it, and the community practice followed in a specific year.

The dossier says the traditional calendar also draws on the 60-year *samvatsara* cycle. It associates Vijayaptham 714 with 2026–27 and predicts the next named year. Treat that identification as a claim in the dossier that merits confirmation against a published calendar or a knowledgeable community authority before using it as a definitive date.

## Keep the record as well as the date

When recording a festival, include both calendar names, the observed date, the location, and the calendar source. Ask families how the date was announced, not only what the date was. Those details can help future readers understand how a tradition moves from printed calendars into lived practice.
